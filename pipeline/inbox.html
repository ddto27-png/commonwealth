Computing / attention · checked 2026-09-21

Daylight

A tablet with a paper-like display for reading, writing and using apps.

Who it serves
Readers and people who want a calmer place to write or take notes.
How technology helps
A reflective display designed for fluid interaction and use in sunlight.
Why it fits Commonwealth

How new is it?
New to Commonwealth, not a newly launched product. The maker's page includes reactions dated May 2024.

Displays / memory · checked 2026-09-21

Looking Glass Go

A portable display for viewing 3D memories and creative work without a headset.

Who it serves
People displaying personal images and creators experimenting with spatial imagery.
How technology helps
Light-field display technology presents multiple perspectives so digital images can appear to have depth.
Why it fits Commonwealth
It gives a digital memory a physical place in a room. It could inspire us to think about how saved ideas are displayed and revisited.
How new is it?
New to Commonwealth; this run did not verify a launch date.
Before featuring
Go is distinct from the company's newer large-format displays. The maker says Go has no included battery; portable does not mean cordless.

Games / physical interaction · checked 2026-09-21

Playdate

A pocket game console with a hand crank that controls actions in some games.

Who it serves
Players and independent developers interested in unusual, small-scale games.
How technology helps
Digital games respond to an analog crank alongside conventional buttons.
Why it fits Commonwealth
A simple physical gesture becomes a creative constraint. It is a strong reference for making Commonwealth's tear-off interaction feel purposeful.
How new is it?
An established reference newly added to our research queue, not presented as a recent startup.
Before featuring
The crank is a controller, not a charger, and not every game uses it. This is the first pipeline calibration batch, not a roundup of new launches.
